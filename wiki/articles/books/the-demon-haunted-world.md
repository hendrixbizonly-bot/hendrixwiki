# The Demon-Haunted World

I came to *The Demon-Haunted World* through [[Carl Sagan]], expecting skepticism. What I did not expect was how carefully he keeps skepticism beside wonder.

The book moves through superstition, pseudoscience, extraordinary claims, and the ways a person can mistake a wanted answer for a supported one. Its value to me is not a list of foolish beliefs held by other people. It is the reminder that my own mind uses the same machinery.

## The candle

Sagan's scientific posture is demanding without feeling emotionally empty. He does not need mystery to be false before the universe can be beautiful. Reality already contains astronomical time, improbable life, and more scale than I can properly imagine.

That is why his argument that science can produce something like spirituality appeals to me. Understanding more does not flatten awe. It gives awe somewhere firmer to stand.

The book now sits beside [[Richard Feynman]] and [[How Easy It Is to Fool Ourselves]] in my mind. Sagan asks how we protect wonder from deception. Feynman asks whether we have made a real attempt to expose our own errors. Together they make intellectual humility feel less like politeness and more like method.
