# Cosmos

I am interested in *Cosmos* less as an old science television series than as evidence that explanation can carry a worldview.

[[Carl Sagan]] moves between cells, trees, stars, and human history without treating science as a pile of disconnected facts. The scales belong to one story. An oak and a person share the chemistry of life. The elements inside both sit inside a longer cosmic history. The universe becomes larger and more intimate at once.

That tone is difficult to get right. Wonder can become vague. Explanation can become cold. *Cosmos* keeps trying to hold precision and feeling in the same frame.

It belongs in this period of the wiki beside [[Astronomy]], [[Pale Blue Dot]], and [[We Come from Stars]]. It is also close to [[Skywardly]], which begins with a much smaller version of the same impulse: look at what is physically there, understand it, and see whether knowing more changes the feeling of looking.
