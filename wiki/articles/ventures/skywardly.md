# Skywardly

## Looking up and wanting an answer

Skywardly began with a very literal question: what am I actually looking at?

I can stand outside, see a bright point above a building, and know that it is probably a planet or a star. That answer is no longer enough for me. I want to know what is above my head right now, what sits near the horizon, what object I am facing, and what becomes interesting once I know its name.

The project used to be called Astro View. Skywardly is the current name.

## Making the sky legible

The app is meant to use my position and the time to make the surrounding sky understandable. Real star data matters here. I am looking at NASA APIs where they are useful, the Yale star catalogue and other astronomical data, then the calculations needed to determine what is overhead and what is close to the horizon.

Some of that is still exploration. I do not want to describe an implementation that does not exist yet. The direction is clearer than the final technical shape.

The important part is that the sky should stop feeling like a flat backdrop. A person should be able to look in a direction and get a grounded sense of what is physically there, then keep learning if curiosity catches.

## Why I keep returning to it

Recently I have been reading much more about [[Astronomy]], [[Carl Sagan]], *[[The Demon-Haunted World]]*, and the perspective behind [[Pale Blue Dot]]. Skywardly belongs to the same period of curiosity, except it asks me to build something from it.

That is what I like about the project. It joins a feeling I already have when I look up with the difficult, ordinary work of data, position, interface, and explanation. Wonder is the beginning of it, not a substitute for making it work.
