# A Belly Full of Ink

I saved a Chinese expression that was translated as “to have ink in the stomach.” I do not know enough about the phrase to make claims about the perfect translation. I like the image anyway.

Reading accumulates somewhere before it becomes available as an argument. One book changes what I can see in the next one. A sentence about astronomy can alter the feeling of a novel. A bad business book can make a good essay look cleaner. A strange piece of fiction can give shape to something I had only understood as mood.

## Before I can explain it

When the accumulation is easy to articulate, I call it knowledge. When it becomes harder to unpack, it starts to feel like intuition.

That does not make intuition magical. Sometimes it is only experience after compression. I notice that an interface feels wrong, a sentence is pretending, or an idea resembles something I read years ago. The reasons may arrive later. The recognition comes first.

This is part of what [[Reading and the Mind]] changes. Reading widely does more than add facts to a shelf. It changes the instrument doing the looking.

## Other eyes, competing books

One lifetime gives me one set of eyes. Books loosen that restriction for a while. I can borrow another mind, including an invented one, and return with a perspective I could not have produced from my own experience alone.

The books do not enter neatly. Good books, bad books, serious books, strange books. They disagree and bump into each other. Bad work teaches taste too. Sometimes I only discover what I value when two influences wrestle long enough for me to choose between them.

I do not need to explain the whole stomach. I just want to keep putting ink in it.
