# The Boredom We Lost

I remember a particular kind of boredom that does not seem to exist anymore.

It usually happened in the back seat of a car. The drive was not long enough to sleep through and there was not much to look at besides trees and houses passing by. After counting enough red cars or tracing raindrops across the window, my mind had to produce something of its own.

I replayed conversations that never happened. I imagined cities somewhere beyond the horizon. I wondered why the Moon seemed to follow the car even when everything closer to us kept disappearing behind it.

Nothing useful came from those moments. Most of the thoughts disappeared forever.

## Nowhere else to go

Looking back, the unusual part was not the boredom. It was that my attention belonged to me because there was nowhere else for it to go.

The empty space lasted long enough to change texture. First it was uncomfortable. Then the mind began throwing things into it. A story, a question, an imaginary argument. Boredom forced imagination to make its own stimulation with whatever happened to be nearby.

That is different from the empty moments I have now. A lift, a queue, the first thirty seconds after sitting down. Each one arrives with an exit already in my pocket.

## Intelligence in the pocket

For most of history, intelligence was not something a person carried around waiting to answer anything. A smartphone changed that relationship. AI changes it again. Information is immediate, and increasingly the first draft of thought can be immediate too.

I am excited by that. I use AI constantly and think it can make learning and building much faster. Still, abundance changes what becomes scarce. Answers are easier to reach. Unclaimed attention is harder.

There is a difference between having no answer and staying with a question. In the back seat, I did not know why the Moon followed the car. I had enough time to build several wrong explanations before I could ask anyone. The wrong explanations are not valuable because they were wrong. What feels missing is the small stretch of mental territory in which they could appear at all.

I do not remember most of what I thought about while the houses passed. I remember the feeling of a mind with nothing available except itself.
