# We Found Each Other in the Cosmos

After Carl Sagan died, Ann Druyan was asked whether he had changed his mind about an afterlife. Her answer stayed with me because she did not try to make the loss easier by adding a reunion she did not believe in.

What mattered to her was that they had found each other at all. In the immensity of space and time, their lives overlapped. They were here at the same time. That was enough to be extraordinary.

## Enough without forever

I understand the wish for something to last forever. The end of a person feels incompatible with the amount of life they contained. But permanence is not the only measure of value.

A relationship can end and still have happened completely. Two people can meet by chance, alter each other's lives, share work and ordinary mornings, then lose the time that held them together. Finitude does not make the meeting less real. It makes the coincidence harder to ignore.

Against the scale of [[Pale Blue Dot]], finding any particular person begins to look wildly improbable. Not supernatural. Just improbable enough.

I like the restraint in Druyan's response. She was not saying death was acceptable or that grief becomes easy when placed against the cosmos. She was refusing to make their love dependent on a second life.

They found each other in this one.
