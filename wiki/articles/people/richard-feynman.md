# Richard Feynman

The part of Richard Feynman that currently interests me is not the mythology of the eccentric genius. It is his suspicion of knowing-looking things.

In his 1974 Caltech address on “cargo cult science,” he described work that copies the outward form of science while missing the discipline that makes science reliable. The instruments, vocabulary, and procedure can all be present. What is absent is the effort to expose the ways the result might be wrong.

That lands uncomfortably well outside laboratories too. A confident explanation can resemble knowledge. A dashboard can resemble measurement. A serious tone can resemble rigor. I am susceptible to appearances like anyone else.

Feynman's harder standard is to give opposing evidence a fair chance and make it easier, not harder, for another person to see the weakness in an idea. Intelligence does not grant immunity from self-deception. Sometimes it only gives the deception better language.

That thought meets [[Carl Sagan]] in [[How Easy It Is to Fool Ourselves]]. They have different temperaments on the page, but both keep returning me to epistemic humility.
